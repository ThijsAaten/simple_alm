## F1 — the §9.2 symmetry claim SURVIVES on the corrected panel, strengthened

| Pair | Pre-2017 | Post-2017 | Fall |
|---|---|---|---|
| China–US | 0.54 (0.50) | 0.21 (0.35) | **−0.33** |
| China–Europe | 0.60 (0.61) | 0.24 (0.36) | **−0.36** |
| AsiaExJP–US | 0.69 (0.67) | 0.56 (0.60) | −0.13 |

| Europe–US | 0.82 (0.78) | 0.77 (0.80) | −0.05 |

The load-bearing sentence stands: China's fall is **0.33 against the U.S. and 0.36
against Europe** — symmetric to within 0.03, and if anything marginally *larger* against
the European anchor, which is the opposite of what a U.S.-policy-contingent decoupling
would produce. The aggregate "fell modestly" claim also survives symmetrically
(0.13/0.16). The general post-2017 dispersion §9.2 cites is real but small (Europe–US
−0.05); the China falls are six to seven times it. In rolling-mean form the falls are
0.15/0.25 — less symmetric, because the 60-month window drags pre-2017 history into the
post-2017 readings; the sub-period simple correlations are the clean statement and the
one the prose should carry.

**The diagnostic hit.** The withdrawn "0.60 unconditionally" is **exactly the computed
pre-2017 China–Europe correlation (0.60)** on the current panel, and the withdrawn
"full-sample tail correlation 0.61" equals the pre-2017 China–Europe rolling mean
(0.61). Both were correctly computed on the corrected panel and **mislabelled as
unconditional/full-sample figures — not USD-era residue**, a different defect than my
Q1 verdict claimed, with different implications: the original scratch computation was
sound and the labels were wrong, which also explains why the −0.06 survived. My Q1
answer's "pre-correction residue throughout" is withdrawn on that point.

## F2 — the companion figures move, and the sentence's strong clause fails

Worst decile of European months post-2017 (n=11, indicative as the paper already says),
with the unconditional post-2017 correlation alongside:

| Holding | Tail corr | Uncond. post-2017 | Holds or rises? |
|---|---|---|---|
| USA | **+0.71** (published 0.80) | 0.77 | no — dips |
| China | −0.05 | 0.24 | no — the unique decoupler |
| India | **+0.90** (published 0.77) | 0.49 | yes — rises sharply |
| Korea | +0.36 | 0.63 | no — dips |
| Taiwan | +0.52 | 0.56 | holds |
| Japan | +0.50 | 0.72 | no — dips |
| Singapore | +0.85 | 0.71 | yes |
| AsiaExJP | +0.43 | 0.59 | no — dips |

The two published companions move: **US 0.80 → 0.71, India 0.77 → 0.90** (one against
the contrast, one for it). The rhetorical core survives — China is the only holding
whose tail correlation collapses to zero, everything else stays strongly positive — but
the clause "every other holding in the sleeve holds or rises in the same months" is
**false as written**: USA, Korea, Japan and the aggregate all dip modestly in the tail.
The honest sentence is that every other holding *remains strongly positively correlated*
(0.36–0.90) while China alone goes to approximately zero. Flagged for the §9.8 rewrite;
not written.
